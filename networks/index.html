A network rents you an audience of publishers and handles tracking and payouts. Software gives you tracking and payouts, and you bring the partners. Here is what each costs, using published prices.

Network or in-house: the short answer

Choose a network when you need partners you don’t have yet. You pay for access to its publishers, usually through a monthly fee plus a share of every commission. That share is the real cost: at 3.5% of order value, a network costs a $120 order about $4.20 on top of the commission itself.

Choose software when your partners already exist: customers, newsletter writers, agencies and creators who already talk about you. At $49 to $150 a month, software is usually cheaper once a program passes a few dozen sales a month, but every partner has to be recruited by you.

Many brands run both: a network for discovery in year one, and software for the partners who become top performers. Our guide to networks versus in-house software works through the break-even math.

For publishers

One account on a large network gives you access to thousands of brands with a single payout. Direct programs pay more often and sometimes better, but each needs its own application and has its own payout threshold. Start with one network that fits your audience, then add direct programs as you learn which products your readers buy.
