Affiliate marketing glossary

Fifty terms you will meet in program agreements, network dashboards and partner calls, defined in plain English.

ABCDEFHILMNOPRSTUV

A

Advertiser
The business that runs an affiliate program and pays commission. Also called the brand or merchant.
Affiliate
A person or company that promotes another business’s products in exchange for commission. Also called a publisher or partner.
Affiliate agreement
The contract that sets commission, payment terms, allowed promotion methods and disclosure rules. Read it before promoting.
Affiliate manager
The person at a brand or agency who recruits, supports and pays partners. A responsive one is worth a slightly lower rate.
Affiliate network
A platform that tracks sales, pays partners and gives brands access to its publishers, such as Awin, CJ or Impact.
Attribution
The rules that decide which partner gets credit for a sale when several were involved.
Attribution window
How long after a click a purchase still counts. See cookie window.
Average order value (AOV)
Total revenue divided by number of orders. Percentage commissions scale with it.

B

Bounty
A fixed amount paid for each qualifying customer or signup, regardless of order size.
Brand bidding
Buying search ads on a brand’s name and sending the clicks through an affiliate link. Most programs prohibit it.

C

Cashback site
A publisher that shares part of its commission with shoppers. Useful for conversion, but often credited with sales other partners created.
Chargeback
A payment reversed by the customer’s bank. The related commission is usually reversed too.
Clawback
Recovering commission already paid when a sale is later refunded or found to be fraudulent.
Click-through rate (CTR)
The share of readers who click a link: clicks divided by views.
Commission
What a partner earns for a qualifying action: a percentage, a fixed bounty or both.
Conversion rate
The share of clicks that become sales or signups.
Cookie
A small file in the browser that records which partner referred a visitor.
Cookie stuffing
Fraud that drops affiliate cookies on people who never clicked a link.
Cookie window
How long a referral stays valid after a click, from 24 hours to the life of the account.
Coupon site
A publisher that lists discount codes. Programs often set special rules or rates for them.
CPA (cost per action)
Paying a fixed amount when a defined action happens, such as a sale or trial.
CPC (cost per click)
Paying per click rather than per sale. Rare in affiliate marketing because it invites fraud.
CPL (cost per lead)
Paying a fixed amount for each qualified signup or enquiry.
CPS (cost per sale)
Paying commission only when a purchase is completed.

D

Deep link
An affiliate link to a specific product or page rather than the homepage. It usually converts better.
Disclosure
A clear statement telling readers you may earn from a recommendation. Required by the FTC and most programs.

E

EPC (earnings per click)
Total commission divided by clicks. The fairest single number for comparing programs. Try the EPC calculator.

F

First-click attribution
Giving credit to the partner who introduced the customer, rather than the last one clicked.

H

Hybrid commission
A mix of models, such as a bounty for the first sale plus a smaller recurring percentage.

I

Incrementality
Whether a partner created a sale that wouldn’t otherwise have happened. Brands increasingly pay more for incremental partners.
In-house program
A program run on the brand’s own affiliate software instead of a network.

L

Last-click attribution
The industry default: the last affiliate clicked before purchase gets all the credit.
Lead
A potential customer who has shared contact details or requested information.
Lifetime commission
A recurring commission paid for as long as the referred customer keeps paying.
Link cloaking
Hiding a long affiliate URL behind a short, clean one on your own domain. It never replaces disclosure.
Locking period
The date after which pending commissions can no longer be changed and are approved for payment. See validation period.

M

Material connection
Any relationship that could affect how people view a recommendation, such as payment, free products or employment. It must be disclosed.
Merchant
Another word for the advertiser or brand.

N

Network fee
What a network charges a brand on top of commission, as a percentage of sale value or of commission, plus any monthly fee.

O

Override
A network’s cut calculated as a share of the commission a brand pays partners.

P

Payout threshold
The minimum balance you must reach before a program pays you.
Postback
Server-to-server tracking that reports a sale directly from the brand’s system to the platform, without relying on browser cookies.
Publisher
A site, creator or company that promotes products as an affiliate.

R

Recurring commission
Commission paid on every renewal of a subscription, for a set period or for life.
Referral program
A program that rewards customers for recommending a product to people they know, usually with credits or discounts rather than cash.
Revenue share
Commission as a percentage of the revenue a customer generates.
Reversal
A commission cancelled because the sale was returned, refunded, unpaid or invalid.
RPM
Revenue per thousand pageviews. Useful for comparing affiliate income with display ads.

S

Server-side tracking
Tracking that records clicks and sales on servers rather than in the browser, so privacy tools and blockers lose fewer sales.
SubID
An extra tag you add to an affiliate link to see which page, button or campaign produced a sale.
Sub-affiliate
A partner recruited by another partner, who may earn a share of their commissions in two-tier programs.

T

Tracking link
A link that carries your partner ID so the program can credit you.
Two-tier program
A program that also pays you a share of commissions earned by partners you referred.

U

UTM parameters
Tags added to a URL, such as utm_source and utm_campaign, that tell analytics tools where a visit came from. Build them with the link builder.

V

Validation period
The time a brand takes to confirm sales before approving commission, often 30 to 90 days.
