Affiliate content that converts

By the Affilia editorial team. Updated November 6, 2026. An 8-minute read.

Affiliate content converts when it helps a reader make a decision they were already trying to make. Traffic matters, but intent matters more: a thousand readers comparing two tools are worth more than a hundred thousand reading general tips.

Match the format to the reader’s question

Reader is askingFormat that answers it
Which is best for my situation?“Best X for Y” guides that name a situation, not just a category
A or B?Head-to-head comparisons with a clear verdict
Is this one any good?Single-product reviews based on real use
What else is there?Alternatives pages for people leaving a product
How do I do this?Tutorials that use the product to get a result

Tutorials deserve more attention than they get. A reader who follows your steps inside a tool is already a user; recommending the paid plan is a small step.

What makes a review believable

Google’s own guidance on reviews asks for evidence of hands-on experience, measurements where they matter, honest drawbacks, and comparison with alternatives. Readers ask for the same things. In practice:

  • Show your own screenshots, photos or results, not the vendor’s marketing images.
  • Give numbers you measured: setup time, speed, cost after renewal, support response time.
  • Say who the product is wrong for. It makes your recommendation for everyone else credible.
  • Name the alternative you would pick if this one doesn’t fit.

Structure a comparison page that sells

  1. The verdict first. One paragraph: which option, for whom, and why.
  2. A comparison table with the four or five criteria that decide the choice, not thirty.
  3. One section per option, each ending with a clear call to action.
  4. How you tested, so readers can judge your method.
  5. An FAQ answering the objections that stop people buying.

Calls to action that respect the reader

Name what happens when someone clicks: “See current pricing on HubSpot” tells the reader more than “Click here.” Put a call to action where a decision naturally happens, after the verdict, after each option, and after the table, rather than scattering links through every paragraph. Disclose before the first link, every time; the disclosure guide explains how.

Keep ads and affiliate offers working together

Display ads and affiliate links can share a page. Lead with the offer where intent is high, keep ads lower down and visually distinct, and never style an ad like a button or a product box. AdSense policies forbid formatting that makes ads look like site content or navigation. The EPC and RPM calculator shows which earns more on a given page.

Update or lose it

Prices, plans and features change. A comparison that was right last year can now recommend a plan that no longer exists. Put a review date on every commercial page and revisit your top earners at least twice a year.
