SEO for affiliate sites in the AI-search era

By the Affilia editorial team. Updated November 6, 2026. An 8-minute read.

Search is still the largest source of affiliate traffic, but the easy part of it has gone. AI-generated answers now handle many simple questions on the results page itself, and Google has spent years tightening how it treats thin commercial content. What remains rewards sites that know things others don’t.

What changed

  • Answers moved onto the results page. Definitions, quick facts and simple how-tos are increasingly answered by AI summaries, so they send fewer clicks.
  • Evidence became the ranking currency for reviews. Google’s guidance on reviews favors firsthand testing, original measurements and honest drawbacks over summaries of other reviews.
  • Spam policies grew teeth. Since March 2024, Google’s spam policies explicitly target scaled content abuse, meaning many low-value pages produced to rank rather than to help, along with site reputation abuse and expired-domain abuse.

What still ranks and earns

Commercial investigation queries. “Best CRM for a ten-person agency” or “Webflow vs WordPress for a portfolio” involve trade-offs a short summary can’t settle. Readers still click through for depth, and their intent is high.

Original data. Speed tests, price tracking over time, survey results, support-response timings. Data you produced is data others cite and link to.

Interactive tools. Calculators, quizzes and comparison filters answer questions in a way a paragraph can’t, earn links and keep readers on the page.

Topical depth. Twenty excellent pages that cover one area completely beat two hundred thin pages across ten areas.

Build evidence into every commercial page

  1. Say who wrote it and why they know the subject.
  2. Show the product in your hands or on your screen.
  3. State how you tested and what you measured.
  4. Give a verdict that names who should not buy.
  5. Date the review and update it when the facts move.

The technical basics still matter

  • Fast, mobile-friendly pages, with no layout shift from late-loading ads or widgets.
  • Affiliate links marked rel="sponsored" or rel="nofollow", as Google asks.
  • Clean internal links from guides to the comparisons and reviews they support.
  • An XML sitemap and a sensible URL structure.

Don’t rely on one source of visitors

A single algorithm update can halve an affiliate site’s traffic overnight. Use search to find readers, then give them reasons to come back through other channels: an email list you own, a YouTube channel, a community. The next guide covers the two strongest: email and YouTube.
