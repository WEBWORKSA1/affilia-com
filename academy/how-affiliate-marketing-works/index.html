How affiliate marketing actually works, and who gets paid

By the Affilia editorial team. Updated November 6, 2026. A 7-minute read.

Affiliate marketing is advertising that only costs money when it works. A brand pays a partner after the partner’s audience does something the brand values: buys, signs up, or becomes a qualified lead. Everything else in the industry is detail on top of that one rule.

The four parties in every sale

  • The brand, also called the advertiser or merchant, sells something and sets the commission.
  • The publisher, also called the affiliate or partner, recommends it: a blogger, YouTuber, newsletter, comparison site, coupon site, app or another company.
  • The customer clicks the recommendation and buys. They pay the normal price; the commission comes out of the brand’s margin.
  • The tracking platform records the click and the sale and makes sure the right partner is paid. It is either a network such as Awin, CJ or Impact, or software the brand runs itself.

What happens between a click and a payout

  1. The publisher places a link that carries their partner ID.
  2. A reader clicks. The platform notes the click and stores a marker, through a cookie, a first-party identifier or a server-to-server record, that says who sent this visitor.
  3. If the reader buys within the program’s attribution window, the sale is matched to the publisher and recorded as pending.
  4. The brand checks the sale during a validation period, often 30 to 90 days, to remove returns, cancellations and fraud.
  5. Approved commissions are paid once the publisher’s balance passes the payout threshold, usually monthly.

That validation step surprises most beginners: a sale you see today may become money two or three months from now, and some of it will be reversed.

Where the money goes on a real order

Take a $100 order from a store paying 6% commission on Awin’s entry plan. The publisher earns $6. Awin charges the brand a tracking fee of 3.5% of the sale value, $3.50, plus a monthly platform fee. The brand’s total cost of that sale is $9.50, or 9.5% of revenue, and the customer pays $100 either way.

Run your own version of this math with the commission calculator, or the brand planner if you are on the paying side.

The common payout models

ModelHow it paysTypical in
Percentage of saleA share of each orderRetail, travel, marketplaces
Fixed bountyA set amount per new customerHosting, SaaS, financial products
RecurringA share of every renewal, for a set time or for lifeSubscriptions and software
Cost per leadA set amount per qualified signupInsurance, education, B2B services
HybridA bounty plus a smaller recurring sharePremium software and hosting

Why both sides use it

Brands like affiliate marketing because spend tracks results: no sale, no commission. It also reaches audiences that trust the publisher more than they trust ads. Publishers like it because they can earn from recommendations without stock, customer service or payment processing. It is a large channel; eMarketer forecasts US affiliate marketing spend above $13 billion in 2026.

What it isn’t

It isn’t passive at the start. Earning requires an audience that trusts you, content that helps people decide, and time for that content to be found. It isn’t a way to get rich quickly, and anyone selling it that way is usually earning their commission from you. Treated as a business, though, it is one of the few models where a single person with good judgment can compete with large companies.
